## 1. Unit Digit (Cyclicity / Unit Theorem)

### Type 1: Digits $0, 1, 5, 6$
The unit digit always remains the same for any positive integer power $n$ ($n \ge 1$):

$$0^n = 0, \quad 1^n = 1, \quad 5^n = 5, \quad 6^n = 6$$

### Type 2: Digits $4, 9$
The unit digit alternates depending on whether the power is odd or even:

| Digit | Odd Power | Even Power |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$4$** | $4^{\text{odd}} = 4$ | $4^{\text{even}} = 6$ |
| **$9$** | $9^{\text{odd}} = 9$ | $9^{\text{even}} = 1$ |

### Type 3: Digits $2, 3, 7, 8$
These digits follow a cycle of $4$. To find the unit digit:
1. Divide the power by $4$ and take the **remainder** as the new power.
2. **Important:** If the remainder is $0$, use **$4$** as the new power.

#### Example: Find the unit digit of $2^{48} + 7^{22} + 6^{44}$
* **$2^{48}$:** $48 \div 4$ gives remainder $0$, so use power $4$. Unit digit of $2^4 = 16$ is **$6$**.
* **$7^{22}$:** $22 \div 4$ gives remainder $2$, so use power $2$. Unit digit of $7^2 = 49$ is **$9$**.
* **$6^{44}$:** Falls under Type 1, so the unit digit is **$6$**.

Add the unit digits:
$$6 + 9 + 6 = 21 \implies \text{Unit digit} = \mathbf{1}$$

## 2. LCM & HCF

### Example 1: Find the LCM and HCF of $70$ and $90$

#### LCM (Least Common Multiple)
```text
2 | 70, 90
5 | 35, 45
7 |  7,  9
3 |  1,  9
3 |  1,  3
  |  1,  1
```

$$\text{LCM} = 2 \times 5 \times 7 \times 3 \times 3 = \mathbf{630}$$

#### HCF (Highest Common Factor)
Stop dividing once there is no common factor other than $1$:

```text
2 | 70, 90
5 | 35, 45
  |  7,  9
```

$$\text{HCF} = 2 \times 5 = \mathbf{10}$$

### Example 2: Find the LCM and HCF of $9, 25, 36$
Prime factorizations:
* $9 = 3^2$
* $25 = 5^2$
* $36 = 2^2 \times 3^2$

* **HCF:** There is no common prime factor among all three numbers, so $\text{HCF} = \mathbf{1}$.
* **LCM:** $2^2 \times 3^2 \times 5^2 = 4 \times 9 \times 25 = \mathbf{900}$.

## 3. LCM & HCF of Fractions

### Formulas
$$\text{LCM of Fractions} = \frac{\text{LCM of Numerators}}{\text{HCF of Denominators}}$$

$$\text{HCF of Fractions} = \frac{\text{HCF of Numerators}}{\text{LCM of Denominators}}$$

### Example: Find the LCM and HCF of $\frac{2}{3}$ and $\frac{3}{4}$
* **LCM:**
  $$\frac{\text{LCM}(2, 3)}{\text{HCF}(3, 4)} = \frac{6}{1} = \mathbf{6}$$

* **HCF:**
  $$\frac{\text{HCF}(2, 3)}{\text{LCM}(3, 4)} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{12}}$$

## 4. Product of Two Numbers

### Formula
$$\text{Product of two numbers} = \text{HCF} \times \text{LCM}$$
$$a \times b = \text{HCF} \times \text{LCM}$$

> **Key Rule:** For any valid pair of numbers, the $\text{HCF}$ must always divide the $\text{LCM}$ evenly, and both numbers must divide the $\text{LCM}$ evenly.

### Example 1: Finding the Missing Number
*Corrected values (note: the original values $\text{LCM} = 40, \text{HCF} = 3, a = 60$ are mathematically impossible because $3$ and $60$ do not divide $40$).*

**Question:** The LCM of two numbers is $180$, their HCF is $3$, and one of the numbers is $60$. Find the other number.

**Solution:**
$$\text{Other number} = \frac{\text{LCM} \times \text{HCF}}{\text{First number}} = \frac{180 \times 3}{60} = \mathbf{9}$$

*(Verification: $\text{HCF}(60, 9) = 3$ and $\text{LCM}(60, 9) = 180$.)*

### Example 2: Find the LCM & HCF of $36$ and $198$

```text
2 | 36, 198
3 | 18,  99
3 |  6,  33
  |  2,  11
```

* **HCF:** $2 \times 3 \times 3 = \mathbf{18}$
* **LCM:** $18 \times 2 \times 11 = \mathbf{396}$
